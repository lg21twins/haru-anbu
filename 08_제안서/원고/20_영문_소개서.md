## 0. 용어 — 여기서 흔들리면 전부 흔들린다

| 한국어 | 영어 | 근거 |
|---|---|---|
| 보호자(가족) | **Family** | 배포된 영문 쇼케이스 표기 |
| 요양보호사 | **Caregiver** | 동일 |
| 의료진(간호사) | **Clinician** | 동일 |
| 어르신(입소자) | **Resident** (산문) / **Patient** (앱 이름) | ↓ 아래 주의 |
| 요양시설 | **long-term care facility** | 국제 표준 표현 |
| 급여제공기록 | **care service record** (법정 서식은 *statutory care record form*) | — |

> **주의 — 이 한 줄은 팀이 결정해야 한다.**
> 배포된 영문 쇼케이스는 어르신을 **"Patient"**로 부른다. 그런데 요양시설 거주자의 국제 표준 표현은
> **"Resident"**이고, 심사위원이 이 분야를 아는 사람이면 "Patient"는 **병원 용어를 잘못 가져다 쓴 것**으로 읽힌다.
> 이 문서의 산문은 **Resident**를 쓰되, 앱 이름(`Patient app`)은 사이트와 맞춰 그대로 뒀다.
> **사이트를 통일할지 여부는 팀 판단.** 통일한다면 영문 쇼케이스 23개 화면을 함께 고쳐야 한다.

**절대 쓰지 않는 표현**

| 쓰지 않는다 | 이유 |
|---|---|
| "AI-powered", "AI-driven" | AI 요약은 **아직 구현되지 않았다.** 설계에 있을 뿐이다 |
| "solution", "platform for the elderly living alone" | 우리는 **독거노인용이 아니다.** 시설 입소자용이다 |
| 검증 안 된 수치 일체 | 한글판 `01_수치검증대장.md`의 인용 금지 판정이 영문에도 그대로 적용된다 |
| "Claude", 특정 AI 벤더명 | 공개 제출물에는 벤더명을 빼고 그냥 AI |

## A. 어워드 출품문 (IDA · UX Design Awards · A' 공통)

### A-1. Project title

```
HARU ANBU — Care Communication for Long-term Care Facilities
```

부제가 필요하면:
```
One record, four ways of reading it.
```

### A-2. Short description (약 50 단어 · 요약란)

```
In a long-term care facility, the caregiver already writes a daily record
required by law. Haru Anbu delivers that single record to three different
readers — the family, the resident, and the clinician — each in the language
and depth they actually need. No new writing. No new burden on the floor.
```

### A-3. Full description (약 300 단어 · 본문란)

```
The problem is not a lack of information. It is that the information never moves.

In a Korean long-term care facility, a caregiver already records what happened
during a resident's day. That record exists because the law requires it — a
statutory care record form under the Long-term Care Insurance enforcement rules.
It is filed, stored, and almost never read by the people who most want to read it.

Meanwhile the resident's family knows nothing. They call. Someone answers the
phone, interrupts care work, and relays a fragment. A 2010 Korean study already
found that caregivers and families hold different expectations of each other
while lacking any effective channel between them. COVID-19 did not create that
gap; it made it visible. Visiting resumed, and the structure stayed the same.

Haru Anbu takes the record that is already being written and routes it. The
family receives one calm daily message — meals, sleep, mood, activity — written
in ordinary language, not clinical shorthand. The resident sees a screen built
for low vision and unsteady hands, showing only today. The clinician sees the
same underlying data as a clinical timeline, ordered by what needs attention.
One act of writing, three ways of reading.

We designed against a specific danger. A system that reports on caregivers
becomes a surveillance tool, and a surveillance tool gets rejected by the floor
no matter how good its interface is. So we deliberately did not design
per-worker input statistics, missing-entry alerts, or writer-to-writer
comparison. The record is a channel, not a performance metric.

Four role-specific apps and one clinician web dashboard: 39 unique screens,
published and openly viewable at haruanbu.site.
```

### A-4. What is honest about the current state (요구되면 적는다 — 요구되지 않아도 적는 편이 낫다)

```
This is a completed design, not a shipped product. The server, authentication,
real-time sync, and the AI summarization layer are not built. We estimate two
to four months of engineering to reach a minimum working system. We say this
in every conversation we have with a facility, because a design that hides
what it cannot do is the thing we are trying to design against.
```

> **왜 이걸 자진해서 쓰나.** 컨셉 부문 심사는 "완성됐다고 우기는 미완성"을 가장 싫어한다.
> 미구현을 먼저 밝히면 **컨셉 부문에서는 감점이 아니라 신뢰**가 된다.
> 다만 **"Concept" 카테고리로 낼 때만** 그렇다. 제품 부문에 내면서 이걸 쓰면 자격 문제가 된다.

## B. KHF 해외 부스 — 30초 영어

```
Hi — we're a student team from Korea. We designed a care communication
service for long-term care facilities.

The idea is simple: the caregiver already writes a daily record because the
law requires it. We take that one record and deliver it to the family, to the
resident, and to the clinician — each in a different form. No extra writing
on the floor.

We're not selling anything. We haven't built the server yet, so we can't even
start today. What we're looking for is someone who'll tell us where this
breaks in a real facility. Could I ask you two questions?
```

**두 질문 (외우는 것은 이 둘뿐)**

```
1. In your market, how does a family currently find out how their parent's day went?
2. If that record went to the family automatically — what would go wrong?
```

> 2번이 목적이다. 해외 사업자에게 **"가족에게 자동 전달"이 어떤 사고를 일으키는지**는
> 한국 시설에서 듣는 답과 다를 가능성이 크고, 그 차이 자체가 제안서를 살린다.

**명함을 받으면 하는 말**
```
May I send you the deck? It's about twenty pages, and I'll send it in English.
```

## C. 영문 메일 (부스에서 명함 받은 곳 · 3일 안)

```
Subject: Haru Anbu — the student team you met at KHF (Seoul, Aug 2026)

Dear [Name],

Thank you for the two minutes at your booth on [date]. You said [한 문장으로
그 사람이 한 말을 그대로 인용]. That has been on my mind since.

We're a three-person design team from Kaywon University of Art and Design in
Korea. We designed Haru Anbu — a care communication service for long-term care
facilities. The caregiver's statutory daily record already exists; we route it
to the family, the resident, and the clinician, each in the form they need.
39 screens, openly published at haruanbu.site (English: haruanbu.site/en).

To be straightforward about where we are: the design is complete, the running
system is not. Server, authentication, and sync are unbuilt — we estimate two
to four months. So this is not a sales email and we are not asking you to adopt
anything.

What we are asking for is smaller. If you have two hours at some point, we would
like to hear from a caregiver and a manager where this design would fail in
practice. That conversation is the single thing our proposal is missing.

If that is not possible, we would still be glad to send you our progress.

With thanks,
Shon Ye-chan
Haru Anbu — Kim Ji-wook · Shon Ye-chan · Ko Hae-eun
[email] · haruanbu.site
```

## D. 영문 1페이지 (인쇄·PDF 첨부용) — 본문

```
HARU ANBU
Care communication for long-term care facilities

ONE LINE
The caregiver writes once. The family, the resident, and the clinician
each read it in their own way.

THE GAP
A family with a parent in a care facility has no reliable way to know how the
day went. They call; someone interrupts care work to answer. Meanwhile a
statutory daily care record is already being written — and almost never read
by the people who most want to read it.

WHAT WE DESIGNED
Family app        One calm daily message. Meals, sleep, mood, activity.
Resident app      Today only. Large type, high contrast, one action per screen.
Caregiver app     The record they already file, restructured for speed.
Clinician app/web The same data as a clinical timeline, ordered by urgency.

39 unique screens · published at haruanbu.site

WHAT WE DELIBERATELY DID NOT DESIGN
No per-worker input statistics. No missing-entry alerts. No writer-to-writer
comparison. A record that reports on the people writing it becomes surveillance,
and surveillance gets rejected by the floor.

WHERE WE ACTUALLY ARE
Design complete. Server, authentication, sync, and AI summarization not built —
two to four months of engineering. We say this first, every time.

WHAT WE ASK
Now:   two hours. A conversation with caregivers and a manager, and your verdict
       on where this breaks. No cost, no personal data.
Later: an eight-week pilot at one facility, at no cost to the facility, once
       the system exists.

Kim Ji-wook · Shon Ye-chan · Ko Hae-eun
Service Design / UX — Kaywon University of Art and Design, Korea
[email] · haruanbu.site
```
