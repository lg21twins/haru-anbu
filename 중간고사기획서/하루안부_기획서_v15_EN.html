05 UX Architecture Conclusions Digital Content Production 03

UX CONCLUSION

Three principles the UX design points to

PRINCIPLE 01

The role is the experience

Devices and IA optimized for each of the three parties—family, patient, care team. The same care data is delivered in a role-appropriate way: families on mobile, patients on tablet, the care team (caregivers & nurses) on app and web. Dedicated per-role screens remove unnecessary navigation and deliver a role-focused experience.

Family app · patient tablet · care-worker app · medical-staff web

PRINCIPLE 02

The flow is the design

A single event—medication done, SOS, a care record—syncs four apps in real time. It's not a one-way notification but a structure where everyone knows at once. The information gap between care team and family disappears.

Care event → real-time sync across 4 apps

PRINCIPLE 03

Simplicity is accessibility

A service lasts only if older adults can send a check-in with a single button. A complex UI is an accessibility failure. Simplicity is another name for care, and the starting point of every design decision.

Single-button principle · an answer to the barrier 67.2% of older adults face

AI intro · a warm first greeting

AI check-in card

One-line reassurance message

"She had a peaceful day — she's resting comfortably" — shown in large type over a soft gradient background.

3

Transition to Home

A blur-fade transition leads smoothly into the home dashboard holding the daily-report card. The entry feels gentle.

4

AI generation flow

Care-team checklist → AI analysis → natural-language summary, then auto-sent to the family app at 6 PM each evening. Today's update arrives without a single phone call, shared accurately.

06 Visual Design Development UI Design · Patient Digital Content Production 03

PATIENT · TABLET

Patient App · large-button-centered UI

Designed around buttons and voice input so even digitally unfamiliar 70-somethings reach family in three taps.
High-contrast colors and haptic feedback minimize errors and improve accessibility for elders.

1

Family-centered tablet home

Family photo messages are shown large, with one-tap calling to three people. Along with a medication-done button and today's schedule timeline, full-width "Press to talk" and "I need help" buttons stay fixed at the bottom.

2

One-touch help request · emergency SOS

A single tap instantly calls the assigned caregiver; on SOS, simultaneous alerts go to the care team and family within 30 seconds. It's designed as a minimal interaction so help can be requested without long sentences.

3

Medication reminders · daily timeline

When a medication reminder arrives, one tap on "I took it" marks it done. Meal, exercise, visit, and bedtime schedules appear on chronological cards so elders can check the rest of their day themselves.

4

Family messages · photo inbox

Family photos and messages are shown large on the big screen. For elders who find replying hard, a "Like" reaction button maintains emotional connection with minimal interaction—contributing to emotional stability.

CAREGIVER · TODAY

Today tab · start care logging

The moment a shift begins, it eases handover and repetitive-response burdens by consolidating recipient status and the next action on one screen.

Today tab (Korean)

It instantly shows shift progress and the next task ("Elder Yeong-ja Park · blood-pressure check"). 2

Global mode (English)

3

Global mode (Chinese)

The same workflow is offered in Simplified Chinese so Korean-Chinese and Chinese caregivers work in the same environment.

06 Visual Design Development UI Design · Nurse · Web Digital Content Production 03

NURSE · DESKTOP

Nurse Web · care dashboard

It cuts repetitive phone responses and paper handovers, handling all-patient status, rounds schedules, and family communication on one screen.
AI-summary briefings and priority alerts speed up decision-making.

1

Desktop dashboard

A greeting—"Good morning, Nurse Kim"—accompanies the morning rounds shown as time-slot care cards (10:30–12:00). It provides KPIs (care completion 68% · medication 12/22 · 3 patients needing attention) and a real-time chat sidebar.

2

AI handover auto-generation

At shift change, AI auto-analyzes the previous worker's records and generates a summary structured into the four SBAR steps (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation). After the nurse reviews it, one click completes it—a fast, accurate handover.

3

SOS alert center · family chat

On an SOS, a red banner appears instantly at the top and jumps to that patient's card. Family 1:1 chat is handled in the right sidebar, responding by message instead of phone calls to reduce the care team's burden.

CARE TEAM · MOBILE

Care Team App · the ward in your pocket

Alongside the nurse web dashboard, the mobile Care Team app puts rounds, alerts, and handover in the clinician's hand at the bedside.
It shares one component library with the caregiver app—separate apps, identical building blocks.

1

AI rounds Priority-sorted

Patients are ordered by AI priority for the round. Each card shows vitals, the clinical evidence, and a recommended action—so the most urgent bed comes first.

2

Unified inbox

Alerts, family questions, and team messages land in one inbox, auto-classified and ready to act on without app-switching.

3

Shift handover

A voice memo is turned into an AI SBAR draft and auto-sorted, so the night-to-day handover captures abnormal vitals and cautions in seconds.
